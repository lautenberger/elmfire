\section{Validation objective and scope}

This case asks whether ELMFIRE can reconstruct the landscape-scale spatial
growth of the \CaseTitle{} through \SimulationDuration{} when driven by the
supplied landscape, weather, moisture, ignition, spotting, and wildland--urban
interface (WUI) inputs.  The comparison evidence is a time-filtered set of active-fire detections from the
Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS).
The case exercises the coupled surface- and crown-fire, level-set, firebrand,
ember-ignition, Monte Carlo, and building-spread processes.  It does not isolate
or verify any single algorithm, and it does not establish predictive skill for
events outside the three received reconstructions.

The scientific question is deliberately two-sided: does the ensemble overlap
the observation-derived footprint, and does it do so without achieving overlap
by excessive fire growth?  Overlap, omission, commission, area, and
probabilistic error are therefore reported together.  The archive provides no
independent, predeclared acceptance thresholds, so completed runs are
\emph{characterized}; they are not labeled PASS or FAIL.

\section{Preprocessing and reproducibility}

Preprocessing verifies the supplied reconstruction and observations by size
and SHA-256 checksum.  It checks that required landscape rasters share a grid, confirms that
the namelist-selected weather rasters contain at least 25 bands, computes input
and observation statistics, and regenerates the four input figures.  The resulting statistics and figures characterize the source data without
modifying the original evidence.

Simulation is followed by analysis of the complete ensemble. The declared
default allocation is 50 Message Passing Interface (MPI) processes, one per
ensemble member, without an additional coordinating process. The original
simulation results remain available for examination.  Reproducible input identity does not by itself guarantee result
reproducibility: each run must also record the actual ELMFIRE revision,
compiler, dependencies, and platform.

\section{Validation metrics and comparison method}

For member $i$, let $\tau_i(\mathbf{x})$ be the time-of-arrival raster and $t_c$
the declared comparison time.  The ensemble burn probability and thresholded
footprint are
\begin{equation}
 p(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
 \mathbf{1}\!\left[0<\tau_i(\mathbf{x})\le t_c\right],\qquad
 P=\{\mathbf{x}:p(\mathbf{x})\ge\ProbabilityThreshold\}.
\end{equation}
For the rasterized observation support $O$, intersection $I=|P\cap O|$, and
valid-cell count $M$, the analysis reports
\begin{align}
 J&=\frac{|P\cap O|}{|P\cup O|}, &
 \mathrm{precision}&=\frac{I}{|P|}, &
 \mathrm{recall}&=\frac{I}{|O|},\\
 F_1&=\frac{2\,\mathrm{precision}\,\mathrm{recall}}
 {\mathrm{precision}+\mathrm{recall}}, &
 R_A&=\frac{|P|}{|O|}, &
 \mathrm{Brier}&=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{\mathbf{x}}(p-y)^2.
\end{align}
Jaccard is a symmetric footprint-overlap summary.  Precision diagnoses
commission/overgrowth, recall diagnoses omission/undergrowth, and $F_1$ balances
those errors.  The area ratio exposes size compensation that overlap alone can
hide.  The Brier score retains ensemble probabilities, although the large
unburned domain can dominate it.  Burned-area growth and conditional median
arrival time add temporal context.  VIIRS detections and their derived hull are
the observational baseline; no previous simulation result is used as truth.

Metrics are calculated only when exactly \EnsembleSize{} uniquely identified
members reach the comparison time and share one grid and CRS.  This completeness
requirement prevents partial runs from being reported as scientific results.
The static aspect raster defines the analysis-domain mask.  Member-specific
NoData cells in time-of-arrival outputs represent cells not burned by that
member; they are therefore retained as zero burn outcomes rather than treated
as a grid-mask mismatch.

\section{Interpretation and uncertainty}

A low Jaccard value with high recall and low precision indicates spatial
overgrowth; high precision with low recall indicates undergrowth or a displaced
trajectory.  An area ratio near one cannot establish spatial agreement.  The
Brier score must be read with the footprint metrics because correct prediction
of the large unburned domain may obscure errors near the fire.

The dominant limitations are incomplete input provenance, an observation
product that samples thermal anomalies rather than perimeter, uncertain hull
sensitivity, unknown event-specific calibration history, and absence of
predeclared acceptance bounds.  Model-form error, uncertain fuels and moisture,
meteorological representation, ignition timing/location, spotting parameters,
and WUI parameters are not separable using these aggregate metrics alone.  The
50 archived perturbations characterize one configured ensemble; they do not by
themselves quantify all epistemic uncertainty.

\section{Conclusions and limitations}

This case characterizes the agreement between a complete simulated ensemble
and a spatial support derived from active-fire detections. Input statistics
describe the forcing, spatial comparisons distinguish omission and commission,
and integrated quantities provide context for the overlap metrics.  Its current
scientific result is \textbf{\Metric{status}}; the accompanying reason states
whether the complete current ensemble supported quantitative characterization.

To support a defensible validation decision, future case owners must supply the
upstream input citations and licenses, exact VIIRS collection/granule and
acquisition provenance, the derivation or literature basis for ignition,
spotting, WUI, and perturbation settings, disclosure of any calibration against
this event, and acceptance thresholds fixed before inspecting the evaluated
run.  Until then, even a completed run is correctly labeled CHARACTERIZED, not
PASS or FAIL.

\section{Verification purpose}

This case tests the urban-to-wildland interface of the Wildland--Urban ELMFIRE
(\WUE{}) model.  It separates three observables that can otherwise be
mistaken for one another: heat emitted by an urban source, heat accumulated at
a wildland receiver, and arrival of the level-set fire front at that receiver.
The reviewed WU-E components are identical to source revision
\SourceRevision{}; this identity claim does not extend to other modified
model components. The processes examined are the building design-fire curve,
urban heat transfer, fuel-dependent velocity selection in the level-set
formulation, and ordinary Rothermel surface-fire spread.

Two urban sources are initialized in otherwise nonburnable surroundings.  One
illuminates a disconnected wildland receiver; the other starts a three-cell
wildland path.  The factorial experiment varies urban peak heat-release rate (HRR) per unit area (HRRPUA) over
\PeakSet{}, wildland spread-rate adjustment factor over \AdjustmentSet{}, and replaces the
middle path cell by a nonburnable barrier.  Terrain is flat and deterministic;
firebrands, crown fire, wind fluctuations, and suppression are disabled.  This
is numerical verification and implementation characterization, not physical
validation.

The precise verification question is: across all eight simulation conditions, does the
pinned implementation preserve the fourfold source-HRR and received-heat
responses, keep the disconnected heat-exposed wildland cell unignited, expose
ordinary wildland velocity to spread-rate adjustment factor, and preserve the nonburnable
barrier?  The separate publication claim that heat alone ignites a wildland
target is reported as \textbf{NOT EVALUABLE} because the pinned source contains
no corresponding target-state transition.

\section{Mathematical formulation and numerical implementation}
Urban-to-wildland coupling is outside the downscaled
building-to-building baseline used by CASE43--46 and CASE48--49. It is retained
as a separate published capability \cite{PurnomoPCI2024,PurnomoIJWF2024}.
The proceedings article (pages 2--3,
equations (2)--(8)) and the
supplied thesis (PDF pages 63--67, printed pages 41--45, equations
(2.81)--(2.95)) describe source heat transfer using
\begin{align}
 \dot q_c &= \frac{\HRR\,A_t}{\Delta x^2}, &
 \dot q_r'' &= \frac{0.3\,\HRR}{4\pi R^2}, \\
 \dot q_t &= \alpha_c\dot q_c+\alpha_r\dot q_r''\Delta x^2, &
 E_t^{n+1} &= E_t^n+\dot q_t^n\Delta t .
\end{align}
Here $A_t$ is the flame-overlap area, $R$ is source--target distance,
$\alpha_c$ is a combustible-area coefficient, and $\alpha_r$ includes target
absorptivity.  The documented ignition idealization is
$E_t\geq\mathrm{FTP}\,\Delta x^2$, giving
$U=\sum\dot q_t/(\mathrm{FTP}\,\Delta x)$.

The implementation performs a related discrete heat calculation.  For every
burning source it scans the configured interaction-band thickness, computes
ellipse-overlap factors, sums transient direct flame contact (DFC) and radiation at each target,
and accumulates both after multiplication by $\Delta t\Delta x^2$.  Target
coefficients come from the building-fuel table even where the target fire behavior fuel model (FBFM)
is wildland.  These arrays are observable as transient heat flux and terminal
total-received-heat rasters.

That heat state is not, however, consumed by a wildland ignition law at the
pinned revision.  The WU-E heat-to-velocity routine is selected only when the
current target has fuel model 91.  A target with a fuel model other than 91
instead follows the ordinary surface-fire path; for Rothermel its no-wind base
velocity contains the explicit multiplier
\begin{equation}
 V_{s,0}=\mathrm{ADJ}\;D\;I_R\xi/(\rho_b\epsilon Q_{ig}),
\end{equation}
where $D$ is the diurnal adjustment.  Consequently, received WU-E heat and
Rothermel front arrival are distinct state variables in this experiment.

\section{Derivation of expected behavior}
Let the low design-fire curve be $H_L(t)=H_0h(t)$, where the common
piecewise-linear shape $h$ grows through \SI{60}{s}, remains fully developed
through \SI{300}{s}, and decays to zero at \SI{600}{s}.  The high table changes
only the amplitude, so
\begin{equation}H_H(t)=4H_L(t).
\end{equation}
The heat-loop geometry and target coefficients are unchanged between a matched
low/high pair.  Both \DFC{} and radiation are linear in instantaneous HRR;
therefore, at the disconnected receiver,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\max H_H}{\max H_L}=4,
 \qquad \frac{E_{t,H}}{E_{t,L}}=4.
\end{equation}
Raster roundoff motivates small relative tolerances rather than exact bitwise
equality.

The limits are preregistered numerical discriminators, not experimental
uncertainties.  The 1-percent source-ratio limit is generous relative to
float32 storage while still detecting a material departure from fourfold
scaling; the 0.1-percent repeat limit is tighter because the paired source
inputs are identical.  The 2-percent accumulated-heat ratio allows repeated
float32 additions over 600 one-second updates.  A 1-s time of arrival (TOA) difference is exactly
one configured solver step.  Finally, $10^{-4}$ m/min would move a front only
0.001 m over the full run, far below a 10-m cell, so it separates a dynamically
positive path from numerical zero without serving as a physical rate of spread (ROS) threshold.

The heat experiment places its wildland receiver three cells downwind and
separates it from the source with two FBFM99 cells.  It lies inside the
five-cell WU-E scan but cannot be reached by the continuous front.  The pinned
algorithm accumulates positive heat there but has no conditional assignment
that changes its arrival time from that heat; its \TOA{} must therefore remain
nodata.  This is an implementation invariant, not acceptance of the published
capability as correct.

In the separate path experiment, FBFM1 cells carry spread-rate adjustment factor=0 or 1.
For spread-rate adjustment factor=0 their ordinary Rothermel base velocity is zero; for
spread-rate adjustment factor=1, the configured dry fuel and \SI{20}{mph} westerly wind give a
positive value.  The open path therefore supplies a direct control on ordinary
surface spread.  Replacing its middle cell by FBFM99 supplies an independent
nonburnable invariant.  No arrival at that named FBFM99 barrier cell is
permitted; the metric does not generalize this single-cell control to every
background pixel.

\section{Verification metrics and acceptance criteria}

unique dump-time simulation specification per simulation condition, a unique final record exactly at
\StopTime{}, and grid agreement for every required GeoTIFF.  It requires
 terminal \TOA{}, spread rate, fireline intensity, total \DFC{}, and total
 radiation, plus complete HRR/DFC/radiation transient series matching every
 dump-simulation specification index on the exact 60 s schedule.  With feedback enabled, it
 also requires exactly 600 logged solver-step timestamps on the configured
 0--599 s sequence before selecting the explicit post-step final dump at
 \StopTime{}.  Any missing,
duplicate, malformed, off-grid, or nonterminal evidence makes the run
\textbf{NOT EVALUABLE}; process exit status is never evidence of success.

\section{Simulation configuration and predicted outcome}
The domain is a \SI{310}{m} square in EPSG:32610, with north-up
\CellSize{} cells and nodata $-9999$.  PHI is a clipped signed-distance field
with exactly two negative urban-source cells and no cell centered on PHI zero.
Both sources use building fuel model 1; every wildland receiver is FBFM1 and
the background/barrier is FBFM99.  Source separation exceeds twice the WU-E
heat band, so the isolated receiver is influenced only by its designated heat
source.  A dormant scheduled ignition at \SI{601}{s} prevents an unrelated
stalled-front shortcut but occurs after the stop time.

\section{Technical interpretation}

The implementation-characterization checks pass: source HRR and isolated
received heat preserve the fourfold amplitude response, ordinary propagation
responds to ADJ, and the nonburnable control remains intact. Yet the isolated
wildland receiver has no arrival despite positive heat exposure. This
combination demonstrates the distinction between heat deposition and a
heat-driven ignition pathway.

The source selects heat-derived spread only for urban FBFM91 targets. Wildland
receivers retain the ordinary surface-fire path. Agreement with this current
behavior is a characterization PASS; it cannot establish the separately
claimed heat-only urban-to-wildland capability. The overall capability verdict
therefore remains NOT EVALUABLE. Missing TOA is shown as missing, never as an
arrival at zero seconds.

\section{Scope and limitations}
This case covers deterministic DFC/radiation exposure from urban FBFM91
sources to FBFM1 receivers on a flat \SI{10}{m} grid, two HRR amplitudes, an
ADJ control, and one nonburnable barrier.  It does not validate heat-transfer
coefficients, material ignition thresholds, physical flame dimensions,
firebrands, crown fire, topography, suppression, or stochastic behavior.  It
also does not claim that lack of a target law is desirable; it records that
current-source fact separately from the published intended capability.

The supplied publications are technical evidence, not executable
specifications.  Their notation and default-value ambiguities, including
combustible-fraction semantics and dimensional interpretation of the
heat-derived ROS equations, require resolution before a future U-to-W
ignition-time test can have a quantitative physical reference calculation.  Spatial and
temporal convergence are outside this case and belong to CASE48\_WGC and
CASE49\_WTC, respectively.

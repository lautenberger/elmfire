\section{Verification purpose}

This case verifies time-step convergence of the intended Wildland--Urban ELMFIRE (WU--E) heat-accumulation and urban-propagation law with a fixed material fire-thermal property (FTP) on one fixed uniform-community grid. It refines the configured step through \TimeStepSet{} for families with low and high heat-release rate (HRR). The 30,000-kJ source branch is retained only as a plotted deviation diagnostic and is not an acceptance boundary.

\section{Mathematical formulation and numerical implementation}
The uniform-community and separation context follows the published WU--E
parametric study \cite{PurnomoIJWF2024}. The detailed intended equations are
documented directly here.
Executable semantics were checked against ELMFIRE spread-rate calculation
and ELMFIRE level-set formulation at source revision
\texttt{a2dfbcdf7220}.  The reviewed components were identical to that revision; this identity claim
does not extend to other modified model components.
For received heat flux from direct flame contact (DFC) and radiation, $q=q_{\mathrm{DFC}}+q_{\mathrm{rad}}$, ELMFIRE forms
\begin{equation}
 E=q\,\Delta t\,\Delta x^2.
\end{equation}
The intended routine uses a fixed material property and
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathrm{TP}}=F_{TP,crit}=5400~\si{\kilo\joule\per\square\metre},\qquad
 U=\frac{60E}{0.3048\,\Delta t\,\Delta x\,F_{TP,crit}}
  =\frac{196.85q\Delta x}{F_{TP,crit}}.
\end{equation}
Thus $\Delta t$ controls numerical integration but not the modeled material
law. Heat is recomputed from every burning WU-E source before the two-stage
level-set update. The pinned source's 30,000-kJ switch, dynamic FTP, and
35-mph divisor are documented deviations expected to degrade convergence.

\section{Derivation of expected behavior}
For a time-convergent observable $Q_{\Delta t}$,
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\Delta t}=Q+C\Delta t^p+o(\Delta t^p),
 \qquad
 p\approx\log_2\frac{\lVert Q_{2\Delta t}-Q_{\Delta t}\rVert}
 {\lVert Q_{\Delta t}-Q_{\Delta t/2}\rVert}.
\end{equation}
The low-HRR family supplies a lower-amplitude heat-integration sequence. The
high-HRR family supplies the time of arrival (TOA) observables used for propagation convergence.
Both must have positive finite sampled heat; neither is classified by an
energy branch in the intended model. ELMFIRE feedback level 1
records every solver-step start time.  The evaluator requires the exact expected
step count and increments within the log's 0.1 s print precision; any adaptive
reduction or incomplete log makes the simulation condition NOT EVALUABLE.

\section{Verification metrics and acceptance criteria}
All eight exact-stop, input-matched simulation conditions are required, with generated namelists, tables, and rasters matching the case specification, complete transient stacks on the common 20 s dump schedule, and a complete logged solver-step sequence. Both HRR families must have positive finite sampled heat. Every expected logged start time from 0 through $t_{stop}-\Delta t$ must be present within 0.051 s, accommodating the log's 0.1 s print precision. For the two finest high-HRR simulation conditions, landmark-TOA and community-ROS changes must be at most 5\%; total-heat change in each heat family must also be at most 5\%. A resolvable nonzero sequence must have temporal order at least 0.5; a roundoff-flat three-level sequence is the explicit alternative, while a flat finest pair after a nonzero coarse change fails as possible stagnation. High-HRR TOA fits require $R^2\geq0.95$.

These are preregistered diagnostic criteria rather than literature-provided
uncertainties.  A 5-percent finest-pair limit is large enough to accommodate
the half-second sampling/TOA quantization yet small enough to indicate practical
time-step stability.  The positive order floor of 0.5 tests contraction without
claiming a formal order for this coupled clipped model, and $R^2=0.95$ rejects
fragmentary propagation profiles before a single ROS is reported.  The limits
are not tuned after execution.

\section{Technical interpretation}

The complete step logs establish that the configured temporal sequence was
executed. The finest high-HRR pair satisfies the relative-change limits, but
the three-finest-level differences do not contract at the required rate.
Practical closeness of the finest pair and a positive observed convergence
order are different requirements; passing the first does not override failure
of the second.

The source's step-energy switch and time-step-dependent replacement of FTP can
change the modeled response as the step is refined. They are credible causes
of poor convergence, but the finite sequence does not isolate a unique cause.
The low-HRR heat changes are particularly small: its order is sensitive to
float32 accumulation and to the evaluator's very tight flat-sequence test.
That row should not independently be interpreted as a physical-model failure.
The high-HRR order checks remain separate, decisive acceptance components.

\section{Scope and limitations}
This case verifies numerical behavior of the current executable against the
intended fixed-FTP law, not empirical material calibration. The sampled
cell-step energy is a source-deviation diagnostic only and does not determine
acceptance. The case does not test explicit street gaps, heterogeneous
structures, wildland transition rules, firebrands, or uncertainty in
design-fire inputs.
